\section{Introduction}

The global transition to remote and hybrid learning environments, accelerated by the COVID-19 pandemic, has fundamentally challenged traditional educational assessment paradigms. While emergency remote teaching provided immediate solutions for educational continuity, it also exposed critical limitations in conventional evaluation methods that were originally designed for face-to-face interactions \cite{Martinez2022}. These challenges have intensified the need for innovative assessment frameworks that are both pedagogically sound and technologically adaptable to diverse learning contexts.

Traditional competency-based evaluation systems commonly employ rigid numerical scales (typically 0-1-2-3 or similar discrete ratings) to assess student performance across various competencies. However, this approach presents significant limitations when applied to the complex, multifaceted nature of learning in remote environments \cite{Chen2024}. Such binary or discrete classifications fail to capture the nuanced progression of student understanding and often result in oversimplified representations of learning achievements. Moreover, the inherent subjectivity in evaluating soft skills such as participation, critical thinking, and collaborative abilities becomes increasingly pronounced when teachers have limited direct interaction with students \cite{Kumar2024}.

The inadequacy of traditional grading systems extends beyond technical limitations to pedagogical concerns. Conventional assessment methods tend to emphasize summative evaluation over formative feedback, potentially undermining student motivation and encouraging surface learning approaches rather than deep, meaningful engagement with content \cite{Anderson2022}. In remote settings, where students face diverse technological, social, and economic challenges, these rigid evaluation frameworks may inadvertently penalize circumstances beyond student control, thereby compromising fairness and equity in assessment \cite{Brown2023}.

Fuzzy logic emerges as a compelling solution to address these multifaceted challenges. Unlike classical binary logic, fuzzy systems can effectively handle uncertainty, imprecision, and partial membership—characteristics inherent in educational assessment contexts \cite{Johnson2023}. By allowing for gradual transitions between assessment categories and incorporating expert knowledge through linguistic rules, fuzzy logic systems can provide more nuanced, transparent, and fair evaluation frameworks that better reflect the complexity of student learning \cite{Garcia2024}.

This article proposes and validates a comprehensive fuzzy logic-based continuous assessment model specifically designed for remote and hybrid learning environments. The model integrates multiple assessment variables—including participation patterns, assignment quality, peer interaction, and self-assessment—through a sophisticated fuzzy inference system that maintains pedagogical relevance while reducing subjective bias. The primary objective is to demonstrate that fuzzy logic can effectively bridge the gap between human educational judgment and systematic assessment automation, ultimately transforming evaluation from a purely grading exercise into a meaningful tool for learning support and student development.
